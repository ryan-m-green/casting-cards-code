1 · What is shipped — the mark in the card body, 10% under the body box, at opacity .03

Each card is the real thing: 75 × 110, banner 5px/6px and a 1px rule, body padded 6px — a 73 × 83 body — with the mark held at inset: 5%; width: 90%; height: 90% and object-fit: contain, repainted white by filter: brightness(0) invert(1) at opacity: .03. That value composites to rgb(21, 31, 39) over the card ground, so the mark reads as a tint rather than a drawing here — sections 2 to 4 lift the same mark and split the geometry apart so the shape is judgeable.

3 · Lifted to 1 — the mark's extent in the shipped 90% box

Aspect ratios are 128:120 (dart), 305:342 (shield) and 338:348 (fall) against a 73 × 83 body, so contain resolves on width for all three and the 10% comes off both sides. Measured against the drawn art rather than the viewBox: the dart paints 53.5 × 49.4 with 9.4px of clearance on the left and 16.9px above, the shield 65.5 × 73.5 with 3.6 / 4.7, the fall 56.9 × 47.0 with 7.7 / 17.8. Nothing is cropped, nothing reaches the banner rule, and nothing goes under the completion check — that check lives in the banner.

4 · The pull-back itself — the old geometry beside the shipped one, both at 1

Left of each pair is the geometry that shipped first, the mark filling the body (inset: 0; width: 100%; height: 100%); right is the shipped inset: 5%; width: 90%; height: 90%. Opacity is 1 on all six so the change is visible rather than a matter of belief. The shield is the one that earns the change: at 100% its art sits flush on the body's left edge — 0px of clearance — and clears the top edge by 0.57px, so it read as bleeding into the card border. Every mark is exactly 10% smaller (ratios 0.8998–0.8999) and its centre moves less than 0.02px, so the shrink is centred by construction.

5 · What .03 amounts to, in numbers

The card ground is rgb(14, 24, 32). A white mark at 3% composites to rgb(21, 31, 39) — about seven steps of 255 on each channel, so the mark is legible as a tint once the eye is told to look but still far under the 10px content's contrast. A black mark (the SVGs' own fill) would land on rgb(14, 24, 32) instead and change nothing at all, which is why the body mark keeps the button mark's white repaint.
